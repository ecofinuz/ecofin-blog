## Our Wants Have No Limit

Almost every person dreams of earning more than they do today, living better, and expanding their opportunities. A football player wants to score more goals every time he steps onto the field. A driver wants to reach the destination faster and more safely. An entrepreneur wants to increase income, a student wants higher grades, parents want to give their child a better education, and an investor wants to earn more from capital.

No matter how much money we have, we keep wanting more.

In short, human wants are unlimited. We always want better, more, and faster results.

But there is one problem: resources are limited - money, time, health, and even human life.

The field that studies how these limited resources can be allocated effectively in the face of our unlimited needs and wants is called [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics).

## Participants In The Economic System

Imagine you have 100,000 soums in your pocket. In the shop, there is chocolate you like, a book you have wanted to buy for a long time, and a new pen. You want all three equally. But your money is enough for only one.
Which one do you choose?

That simple question is the starting point of an entire science: economics.
However serious it may sound, each of us is actually an inseparable part of the economy. How?

Think back for a moment: yesterday morning you woke up, had breakfast, went to school or work, and used your phone. An ordinary day, right?

But behind these ordinary actions, an entire economic system is hidden:

- The bread you ate was produced and bought by someone.
- You paid a fare because you went to school or work by bus or taxi.
- Your phone used electricity, and someone paid for that energy too.

All of this is the exchange of money, goods, and services. These processes are the living form of economics in our daily life. In other words, whatever work we do, all of us live as part of an economic process.

## Fundamental Concepts In Economics

When economists explain and analyze the economy, they often use different assumptions, simplified concepts, and models. One of them is that society has two main economic participants:

- Producers ([firms](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firm_(economics)>));
- Consumers, meaning households and people.

Firms use resources that belong to people - land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurial ability - to produce goods and services. Then they sell those goods and services back to consumers.

To earn income, firms [supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand) the goods they produce or the services they provide for other members of society to buy. In turn, people [demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_and_demand) those goods and services because they need them for everyday life. These two concepts - **supply and demand** - form the foundation of economics...

## Branches Of Economics

As a science, economics is divided into two large branches.

The first is [microeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microeconomics), which studies decisions at the level of one person, one shop, or one firm. For example, how much a student spends on breakfast or at what price a farmer sells a product are micro-level questions.

The second is [macroeconomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics), which analyzes processes at the level of an entire country, such as the general price level, unemployment, and the country's total income, **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**. These two branches complement each other. Millions of small decisions people make every day, the micro level, eventually combine and shape the overall picture of the country's economy, the macro level.

In short, economics is not only an abstract science discussed by bankers, government offices, and ministries. Economics is the field that studies the choices we make every day.
