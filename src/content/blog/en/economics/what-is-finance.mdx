Imagine the beginning of an ordinary month. Your salary arrives in your account. You are happy.

Not long after, an SMS comes to your phone: the loan payment has been deducted automatically. A reminder pops up: "We need to buy groceries for home." Then your mobile operator notifies you that tomorrow the subscription fee for your phone number will renew. Utilities, transport, medicine, and other expenses are still waiting.

Most of your monthly salary has gone together with these small messages. The month has just started, but the salary is almost gone. What is happening?

Have you ever thought about how these ordinary processes are connected to finance?

The answer is simple:

> Earning money is a skill; spending it is an art.

Most of us have the skill to work and earn money, but we do not know the art of using money wisely well enough. Our monthly salary is the fruit of our skill, but to manage the money we earn properly, we need to learn another art: finance.

[Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance) is the field that teaches how to earn money, manage it properly, save it, and spend it purposefully. Finance does not mean only banks, [loans](/en/loan/how-loans-work/), or [interest](/en/loan/simple-and-compound-interest/).

At this point, many people ask: "Are finance and economics similar fields?" The answer: **yes, they are similar**, but they are different concepts.

## Finance And Economics

In a broad sense, **finance is part of the field of economics**. It studies how money is formed, how it moves, and how it is managed.

Economics analyzes how limited resources are allocated among unlimited needs. Finance studies how money is raised, distributed, and managed effectively within those processes.

## Branches Of Finance

By scale, finance is usually divided into three main branches: **personal finance**, **corporate finance**, and **public finance**.

For example, [economics explains through supply and demand](/en/economics/what-is-economics/) why the prices of fruits such as apples, pomegranates, peaches, and others rise in winter. But whether you buy those more expensive fruits, how much you buy, or whether you choose another product entirely is a personal finance decision.

Weddings, loans, cars, renovation, and education are all examples of personal financial decisions.

In corporate finance, companies make important financial decisions. For example, how much profit to reinvest in business expansion, how much to distribute to shareholders as dividends, or whether to take a loan for a new project are all corporate finance questions.

Entire finance teams, including accounting, sales, analytics, budgeting, and the board of directors, work on these decisions.

Public finance studies how the state manages its income and expenses. Allocating tax revenue to education, healthcare, or infrastructure, providing subsidies, and funding programs such as 1% cashback are examples of public finance.

Why do we pay taxes? To preserve peace, support the military, run hospitals, and repair roads.

In addition, finance also includes areas such as international finance and financial markets and institutions. These fields study financial relations between countries, currency markets, and the movement of capital.

## Financial Literacy

When people talk about finance, many immediately imagine skyscrapers, investors in expensive suits, stock trading, and huge amounts of money. That is because finance is often imagined in society as something connected to wealth and big business.

> I enrolled my son in math and English classes; when he grows up, he will become a banker.

Many people are familiar with this kind of phrase.

A suit is often seen as a symbol of professionalism, responsibility, and trust. Skyscrapers represent large companies, innovation, and high-level services. But the true meaning of finance is not limited to big money or famous financial centers.

Real financial knowledge is not shown by looking rich. It is shown by [managing money consciously](/en/loan/when-debt-works/). Finance is not only about how much money we earn, but about what opportunities we can create with the money we earn.

Of course, not everyone has to become a financier for our society to develop. But each of us needs to be financially literate. This project exists exactly to promote that financial literacy.
