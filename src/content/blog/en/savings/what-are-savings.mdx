As the logical continuation of credit, we will now focus on [savings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saving). If credit means taking money from a bank at interest, savings means putting extra money aside for tomorrow.

Both are built on the same mathematical and economic ideas: [simple and compound interest](/en/loan/simple-and-compound-interest/), [inflation](/en/loan/inflation-and-loan/), [opportunity cost](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/#opportunity-cost), personal discipline, and more.

The small amount of money left in your hand at the end of the month is not savings. Dollars sitting in a chest are not automatically savings either. Savings have their own conditions, and if you save skillfully, you take a major step toward financial freedom.

In this article, we will look at what savings are and their general characteristics. Treat this article as an introduction.

## What Are Savings?

In simple terms, savings are extra money set aside for the future:

> Savings are the part of income left after expenses and accumulated for future needs or for a specific purpose, including money and material resources set aside for later use.

In economics, savings are expressed with a simple formula:

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">Savings = Total Income - Total Expenses</p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        Savings are the part left after total expenses are subtracted from total
        income
    </p>
</div>

For example, you earn 10,000,000 soums per month and spend 8,000,000 soums on expenses. The remaining 2,000,000 soums are your savings.

But as you can see, this definition is not complete. Savings are not the money left over at the end of the month, because for many people there is no money left over. Without a clear plan, expenses always appear. Even if your monthly income rises from 10,000,000 to 20,000,000 soums, your unplanned expenses also grow proportionally.

Our people have a proverb for a reason: **If it is little, it is enough; if it is much, it disappears.**

Savings are not simply money left after expenses. They are money and wealth assigned to a clear purpose and accumulated according to a plan. They give you confidence in the future, financial security, and freedom.

## Savings And Investment

Savings and investment are close concepts, but they are different terms:

- **Savings** are safe, [liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity), and low-return. A bank deposit, cash, a reserve on a card, or dollars. The purpose of savings is to preserve money for the future and have the ability to use it when needed.
- [Investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment) is risky, less liquid, and higher-return. Investments usually include company shares, real estate, land, government bonds, and precious metals. The purpose is to put idle money to work and earn a profit.

To invest, you first need money, meaning savings. Investments are usually risky and less liquid. If you suddenly need money tomorrow, you may have to sell investments and take a loss.

In practice, investment and savings often mix together. In many individual cases, the difference between the two is not that large.

In the [credit series](/en/loan/simple-and-compound-interest/), we discussed simple and compound interest and their growth patterns. Investment and savings follow the same rules.

## How Should You Save?

So, how is simple saving managed? Usually, in Uzbek families, on payday money is set aside for primary expenses such as food, medicine, household needs, transport, and so on. Debts and contracts are paid: tuition, rent, credit. A certain amount is left for living. The remaining money is converted into dollars and placed in a chest. Our family does the same.

The amount saved changes from family to family. A young family with many children may not save money at all. People aged 35-40 may save a lot for their children's education and future family life. As people approach retirement age, they move from saving money to using it. May everyone reach that stage in good condition.

No matter how much you save, the saving process is very simple:

1. **Separate account.** There should be a separate account for saved money. Converting it into large-dollar bills and putting it in a chest is a common method among our people. What the eye does not see, the hand does not touch. If the account becomes large, you hesitate to break it.

2. **Automation.** On payday, a fixed amount should automatically go into savings. To a bank account, a card, or dollars at home. This is the most important step. If you have money, expenses will appear. By putting money into savings first, you avoid spending it here and there without purpose.

3. **Amount.** How much should you save? As much as you can. Someone puts aside $50, someone $100, and someone else $1,000. [Finance gurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru) usually call people to save 10%-20% of monthly income.

When it comes to savings, it is like exercise. Walking half an hour a day is already very good. Ten or twenty squats are not bad either. Gradually, you may start going to the gym and working with a trainer. The important thing is simply to start. Then, depending on your circumstances, you can keep increasing your savings.

## How Much Should You Save?

There is a popular rule among Western financial advisers and Uzbek finance gurus who follow them: your savings should cover at least **3-6 months of expenses**. This rule may work in America or Europe, but it is hard to apply in Uzbekistan.

For example, if you spend 6,000,000 soums per month, your reserve should be between 18,000,000 and 36,000,000 soums. That is about $1,500 to $3,000. It looks like a lot. And it really is a lot. Finding and saving that amount is difficult.

For many people in Uzbekistan, savings are held in livestock. If you have four sheep at home, that means you have around 15-20 million soums in savings. For someone living in the city, savings may only be money and a car.

For that reason, it is impossible to name one exact number. In any case, you do not have to reach the 3-6 month reserve immediately. It is a goal. Start with a small amount every month. After savings are spent for their purpose, such as a home, car, furniture, or household appliances, you start again.

Our finance professor at Westminster often told us to save at least $50-$100 per month. So that we would not fall into credit, would keep control of our finances, and would have money nearby.

Why are we talking about savings equal to 3-6 months of expenses? Imagine this: you lose your job, get sick, or face an unexpected large expense. This is life; nobody knows what will happen tomorrow. Three to six months of expenses is usually enough money for emergencies.

If your savings are large enough, you will not fall into debt during that time, you will not go to a moneylender, and you will not [take credit](/en/loan/5-steps-before-taking-a-loan/). You will not bother your relatives. You will make decisions calmly, not under pressure. You will not fall into stress and raise your blood pressure either.

From this point of view, savings, however small, are freedom.

## Conclusion

In this article, we looked at what savings are, how they differ from leftover money, how they differ from investment, and how the saving process works. Remember: savings are not leftover money. They are money accumulated for a clear purpose, and they require discipline.

Savings are very useful. For the mind and for health. They give you three things:

- **Security** — when disaster happens, you are financially ready.
- **Freedom** — you are not forced to stay in a job you dislike or in bad conditions.
- **Time** — you make important decisions without rushing.
