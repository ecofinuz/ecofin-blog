## What Is Interest?

**Interest** is a mathematical term that means one part out of 100. 20% means 20 out of 100.

In finance, interest is the "price" of money. A bank, microfinance organization, or lender charges interest for letting you use money. If I give you 10,000,000 soums and ask you to return 12,000,000 soums after one year, the [loan rate](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/) is simply 20%. In other words, you are returning the original 10,000,000 soums plus 20%, or 2,000,000 soums.

In practice, banks calculate loan interest in other ways: [annuity and declining balance methods](/en/loan/how-loans-work/).

The important point is that, in modern finance and economics, interest is simply the price of credit. You use money and pay for it as a percentage. Some people compare interest to rent.

Whether that is right or wrong is a topic for later posts. For now, this is how the modern banking system works.

Western finance is built on the idea that **money has a time value**. Today's 1,000,000 soums is preferred to 1,000,000 soums a year from now, because you can put the money into a deposit today and earn extra income. That is why the concept of interest exists.

There are two main types of financial interest: simple and compound. They are fundamentally different, but both are widely used in the banking system and financial markets. This post is about the difference between them.

## Simple Interest

**Simple interest** is interest calculated only on the principal amount. In other words, the interest amount does not change because the **principal** does not change.

This is the type of interest most people know and understand. [Almost all informal moneylenders](/en/loan/credit-is-not-neutral/) lend this way. For example, 40%. If you borrow $1,000 today and return it tomorrow, you still return $400 on top.

Bank deposits in Uzbekistan often work this way. For example, if you place 10,000,000 soums into Turon Bank's [Imkoniyat plus](https://bank.uz/uz/deposit/imkoniyat-plyus) deposit at 18%, your deposit earns 1,800,000 soums in one year. Turon Bank transfers 150,000 soums to your card every month.

The principal you placed in the deposit remains unchanged. Every year, this deposit type earns the same amount of interest.

Mathematically, simple interest is represented by a linear function (y = ax + b):

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">A = P × (1 + r × t)</p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        P - principal &nbsp;·&nbsp; r - annual interest rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; t -
        number of years &nbsp;·&nbsp; A - final amount
    </p>
</div>

### Example

For example, you place 10,000,000 soums into a simple-interest deposit at a 20% annual rate for 5 years. Your annual income and total savings would look like this:

|                        |                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Annual interest        | 10,000,000 x 0.20 = **2,000,000 soums**   |
| Total interest, 5 years | **10,000,000 soums**                     |
| Final amount           | **20,000,000 soums**                      |

## Compound Interest

**Compound interest** is a system where interest earns interest. In other words, your money "gives birth" to more money. Unlike simple interest, the calculated interest is added back to the principal.

That is why the interest calculated in each period becomes larger. The base for the next period becomes a larger amount.

You may know the book [Atomic Habits](https://asaxiy.uz/product/zheims-klir-atom-odatlar). Habits are similar to compound interest. One week in the gym does not change much, but every workout adds strength and gradually makes you stronger. Bad habits work the same way: playing video games once or twice a month may not be a problem, but every session can make the habit stronger.

In other words, each repeated action helps good or bad habits appear and become part of our character. Compound interest works on a similar principle.

In Uzbekistan, compound interest is called [capitalization](https://finlit.uz/uz/services/question_answer/deposits/?CODE=capitalization-of-interest). Finlit.uz explains it this way:

> **Interest capitalization** is when the interest calculated on a deposit over a certain period is added to the principal amount, and the next interest is calculated on the new amount. In other words, interest is also paid on interest.

As you can see, capitalization is more beneficial for the person placing a deposit. However, many banks do not offer capitalized deposits. But penalties for late payments are capitalized. The bank does not leave even one soum behind.

Mathematically, compound interest is written as an exponential function (y = a^x):

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">
        A = P × (1 + r)<sup>t</sup>
    </p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        P - principal &nbsp;·&nbsp; r - annual interest rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; t -
        number of years &nbsp;·&nbsp; A - final amount
    </p>
</div>

### The Same Example

Let us return to the same example: 10,000,000 soums, a 20% annual rate, and a 5-year term. But this time with compound interest. In banking terms, a capitalized deposit.

A = 10,000,000 x (1.20)^5 = **24,883,200 soums**

|                | Simple interest       | Compound interest     |
| -------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Final amount   | 20,000,000 soums      | 24,883,200 soums      |
| Total interest | +10,000,000 soums     | +14,883,200 soums     |

Same money, same rate, same term, and the same risk. The difference is 4,883,200 soums. That difference grows as time passes or as the interest rate rises. Mathematically, compound interest grows _exponentially_.

## Conclusion

Interest is the price of money - the price of using money for a period of time. Western specialists often call it a rental rate. You receive money and return extra on top. But this is a controversial topic. For example, would you borrow one loaf of bread from your neighbor and return one and a half loaves a month later?

In this article, we only covered the mathematical interpretation of interest. Interest is a much deeper topic.

In short, simple interest is interest where the principal does not change. In compound interest, the principal changes. If interest makes money "give birth" to money, compound interest makes it "give birth to twins."
