## What Is Interest?

**Interest** is a mathematical term that means a part out of 100. 20% means 20 out of 100. In finance, interest is the "price" of money. A bank, microfinance organization, or lender charges interest for letting you use its money. If I give you 10,000,000 soums and ask you to return 12,000,000 soums after one year, the [loan rate](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/) is 20%. You are returning the original 10,000,000 soums plus 20%, or 2,000,000 soums.

In finance and economics, interest is simply the price of using money. You use the money, then pay a fee for it in percent. In one sense, interest works like rent. Whether that is right or wrong is a topic for later posts. This is just how the modern banking system works.

Western finance is built on the idea that **money has a time value**. That is why the concept of interest exists.

There are two main types of financial interest: simple and compound. They work differently, and both are widely used in banking. This post is about the difference between them.

## What Is Simple Interest?

**Simple interest** is interest calculated only on the principal amount. The amount is the same every year. Interest is not added to the principal; it is paid or calculated separately. Your interest payment does not change, because it is tied to a **fixed principal amount**. This is the type of interest most people already understand. [Almost all informal moneylenders](/en/loan/credit-is-not-neutral/) lend this way.

Many bank deposits in Uzbekistan work like this. For example, if you put 10,000,000 soums into a National Bank deposit at 24%, the bank transfers the same interest amount to your card every month: 200,000 soums. The principal you placed in the bank stays unchanged.

Mathematically, simple interest is represented by a linear function (y = ax + b):

```text
A = P x (1 + r x t)

P - principal
r - annual interest rate
t - number of years
A - final amount
```

### Example

10,000,000 soums, 20% annual rate, for 5 years.

|                        |                                           |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Annual interest        | 10,000,000 x 0.20 = **2,000,000 soums**   |
| Total interest, 5 years | **10,000,000 soums**                     |
| Final amount           | **20,000,000 soums**                      |

## What Is Compound Interest?

**Compound interest** is a system where interest earns interest. In other words, your money "gives birth" to more money. At the end of each period, the calculated interest is added to the principal, and the next interest calculation is based on a larger amount.

You can compare it to habits. Going to the gym for one week does not change much, but every workout adds strength and gradually makes you stronger. Bad habits work the same way: playing video games once or twice a month may not be a problem, but every session can make the habit stronger.

In Uzbekistan, some banks call this capitalization. For example, Asia Alliance Bank describes capitalization as adding the interest calculated for a period to the principal and using that new amount for the next calculation. Many banks offer this, but it is usually not the default option. You have to look for it separately.

Mathematically, compound interest is written as an exponential function (y = a^x):

```text
A = P x (1 + r)^t

P - principal
r - annual interest rate
t - number of years
A - final amount
```

### The Same Example

10,000,000 soums, 20% annual rate, for 5 years.

A = 10,000,000 x (1.20)^5 = **24,883,200 soums**

|                | Simple interest       | Compound interest     |
| -------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Final amount   | 20,000,000 soums      | 24,883,200 soums      |
| Total interest | +10,000,000 soums     | +14,883,200 soums     |

Same money, same rate, same term. The difference is 4,883,200 soums. That gap grows as time passes or as the interest rate rises.

## Conclusion

Interest is the price of money - the cost of using money for a period of time. Western specialists often describe it as a rental price. You receive money, use it, and return more than you borrowed. But this is a controversial topic. For example, would you borrow one loaf of bread from your neighbor and return one and a half loaves a month later?

Simple interest is interest where the principal does not change. In compound interest, the principal changes. If interest makes money "give birth" to money, compound interest makes it "give birth to twins."
