We are finishing the credit series. We started with [interest](/en/loan/simple-and-compound-interest/) and [inflation](/en/loan/inflation-and-loan/), then looked at [how credit works](/en/loan/how-loans-work/), its [components](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/), its [cost](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/), and its [essence](/en/loan/credit-is-not-neutral/).

Whether we like it or not, credit is an inseparable part of the modern economy. That will not change anytime soon. [Islamic finance](https://islommoliyasi.uz/uz/) is an alternative to modern finance, not a direct replacement for it. Accept that correctly.

For that reason, I want to end the series with a practical article. If you are going to take a loan, answer the five questions below. This post is not theory. It is a practical step-by-step guide.

## At the Beginning

People usually take credit for this reason: they need something, they do not have the money, and a bank or installment company offers an easy loan without many extra questions. The loan agreement is signed, you receive the money, and then you keep paying interest every month.

Unfortunately, many people do not ask themselves five important questions before taking a loan:

1. Why am I taking this loan?
2. How much am I borrowing?
3. How will I repay it tomorrow?
4. Is there an alternative to credit?
5. From which bank, and under what conditions, am I borrowing?

There is no calculation. No research or comparison. No questions at all. Later, at the beginning of every month, the thought ["this loan again"](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/#psychological-cost) appears.

Answering these five questions changes the process of taking a loan significantly. It does not take long - only about an hour. But that one hour can make the next several years of your life much easier.

## 1. Why?

First, the most important question: **what is this loan for?** Why do I want to take this loan, and where will I spend it?

Is it for construction, furniture, a wedding, a car, or a home? For equipment you need at work? For education and improving your skills? Why are you taking credit? Can you give a clear answer?

Your honest answer should fit one of these:

- **Useful:** Will this money create more money, knowledge, or opportunity for me in the future?
- **Emergency:** Health, safety, or another problem that cannot be solved without money.

If your answer is "I just need it" or "everyone has one" - stop. This loan is not useful for you at all.

Some may say, _But I need a washing machine, gas stove, or fridge at home!_ I agree, of course. But is waiting a few months, saving money, and buying it without owing anyone not an option? Would avoiding credit with three or four months of patience not bring both financial and mental relief?

We discussed this in detail in the [previous post](/en/loan/when-debt-works/). We looked at useful and harmful credit. Go back to that post and compare your own situation.

Then ask the final question: **how useful will this loan be for me in three years?** If the answer is not clear, this step is not finished yet.

## 2. How Much?

All right, you need money, and you have a clearly good purpose. Now ask yourself the second question: **how much money do I need?** If you do not know how much money you need, your goal is still not clear.

Many people skip this question without thinking. Suppose a bank offers you 30 million soums based on your [credit history](/en/loan/credit-score/), income, and property. But according to your own calculation, 18 million is enough. Then take a loan for 18 million soums.

The rule is very simple: **do not borrow more than you need.**

The reason is simple too: you can lose a lot of money by repaying an amount you did not need with interest. As we saw in the [interest calculator](/en/loan/simple-and-compound-interest/), the larger the loan amount, the larger the interest amount becomes.

The difference between 30 million and 18 million is not only 12 million. It is also the extra interest paid over the years, plus [opportunity](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/#opportunity-cost) and [psychological](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/#psychological-cost) costs.

Calculate clearly: how much do you need? Borrow only what you need.

## 3. How Will I Repay It?

Banks, Uzum Nasiya, and stores want you to borrow and spend money, because that benefits them. In ads, they present credit in a [beautiful form](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/#psychological-cost):

> You are your dreams 🚀 Big dreams - made for everyone - continue to grow across our products, partnerships, and initiatives, helping more people get closer to what they aspire to. Because the scale of your life is shaped by the scale of your dreams. [Source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tbcuzbekistan_tbcbank-bigdreams-brandplatform-activity-7444395589125017600-1kFZ?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAD7x8YYB_2oUZlu5ES3JORHZDmJvM3ANFQ0)

Toshmat, who bought an iPhone 18 Pro Max on credit, does not think about how he will repay the loan tomorrow. He needs the phone, and he gets it. Credit providers use strong psychologists to influence our subconscious.

He takes the loan. Many people do not even read the loan agreement. If you talk to acquaintances who work in a bank's collections department, you will hear very interesting and sad stories.

So first, **calculate the monthly loan payment.** Use the [loan calculator](/en/loan/how-loans-work/). Know the exact numbers. Audit your own situation.

Then ask yourself: **what percentage of my monthly income will go to loan payments?** What will you do if an emergency expense appears tomorrow? The bank does not care; whether you are alive or dead, it will collect the interest payments.

One more question: **what happens if I lose my job?** Do I have enough reserve money to cover two or three months of loan payments? If not, this risk is very large.

## 4. Is There an Alternative?

Credit is a distorted form of debt. Debt that is given, taken, and used for the right purpose is not bad in itself. Borrowing from friends, neighbors, and relatives is normal. There is nothing shameful about it.

Credit must be the last resort. Before reaching that decision, have you considered other alternatives? For example:

- **Relatives and friends.** No interest, no penalties, no bank. If there is trust, this is the best option. Of course, debt can damage relationships, but if you return it on time, trust will not crack.
- **Savings.** If the money is not needed urgently, can you wait until you save the required amount? Maybe one month, maybe three months, sometimes six months fits your situation. Be patient.
- **Selling.** Almost everyone has livestock or old things at home that can be sold. An old phone, electronics, sheep, a cow, fruit. Is selling them not an option?
- **Advance payment.** Some companies agree to give a large advance. Usually these are private companies. This is also an option.

If none of the above works, only then does credit make sense. Even then, from an official bank, not a local moneylender.

## 5. Is There a Better Option?

Putting the cap aside, you thought through the questions above. Still, nothing worked, and you decided to take a loan. Now the final step: **find a cheaper loan.**

Many people take the first online loan offered to them. This is a big mistake. Yes, this includes dear TBC and the unicorn Uzum.

Here are good practices for finding the better loan:

- **Compare several banks.** In Uzbekistan, every bank has its own rates, conditions, hidden fees, and penalty terms. The differences can be large. You can find information freely in the loan section of [Bank.uz](https://bank.uz/uz/credits).

- **Look at the [annual percentage rate](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/) (APR), not the monthly rate.** Some banks advertise "3% per month." That means 36% per year or more. Another bank may offer 20% per year. Over the years, the difference turns into serious money.

- **Ask about hidden fees.** Insurance, service fees, early repayment penalties. All of these increase the [true cost of the loan](/en/loan/the-true-cost-of-a-loan/). Read every line before signing the document. Tomorrow, every clause may work against you.

- **Pay attention to the loan term.** If the term is long, the monthly payment is smaller, but total interest becomes larger. If the term is short, the monthly payment is larger, but total interest is smaller. The decision is yours. But calculate both.

## Conclusion

Finally, the best advice: simply do not take credit. Try to stay away from it. Your household will be stable, and your mind will be at peace.

In short, the five steps before taking a loan are:

1. **Why?** - if it is not useful or urgent, do not take it
2. **How much?** - do not borrow more than you need
3. **Can you repay it?** - compare the monthly payment with your income
4. **Is there an alternative?** - credit is the last resort
5. **The best terms** - compare several banks

A person who answers these five questions honestly will have thought about credit much more than the average person. And that difference, over the years, is very large.
