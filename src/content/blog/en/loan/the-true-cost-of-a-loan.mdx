You took a loan from a bank. You see the interest rate, the principal, and the repayment term. The terms might be 20 million soums at 36% for one year. You do a rough calculation in your head. Then you sign the documents and receive the money. You think the calculation is over, and you move on with your life.

But the cost of a loan is not only its interest. Many people never think about this. Of course, many people also do not talk about it. In reality, the true cost of the debt you take has three parts: **money cost**, **opportunity cost**, and **psychological cost**.

Let us look at them one by one and see how much a loan really costs your life.

## Money Cost

A loan has three main parts: **loan term**, **interest rate**, and **principal**. For example, a 100 million soum car loan for 3 years at 28%.

When people talk about the cost of a loan, they usually mean these interest payments. You borrow money, return more than you borrowed, and the difference becomes the bank's income. This number is easy to see in documents, advertisements, and websites. That is why the financial cost of a loan is easy to calculate.

But there are other costs of a loan that most people ignore. For the borrower, there are costs from the side of _opportunity_ and _psychology_. When analyzed properly, their value is not smaller than the financial cost.

## Opportunity Cost

Loans are widespread in our society. Everyone probably has a friend or acquaintance like this.

> Lend me some money until payday. I need to close my loan payment. Otherwise there will be a penalty, and the bank will start looking for me.

Every month, when the payment date comes, you must pay the loan. First. After covering basic needs - food, clothing, transport, utilities - the loan is paid first. Otherwise, the bank charges penalties. Employees call you. If you still do not pay, the case can be sent to the enforcement bureau, and collection may reach the court. That is how the law works.

This process is an example of **opportunity cost**. How? Every family or individual has money they can control each month. Some have more, some have less. And each month, that money can be spent on many good things. If your obligations - living expenses and the loan - increase, the money you can freely use decreases.

For example, suppose you took a car loan for a BYD Yuan Up and must pay 5 million soums on the 10th of every month. Let us say that after basic needs, you have 10 million left. Automatically, 5 million soums goes to the loan.

Think about it: what else could you use that 5 million for? If you think about it, many things. Better education for your child. A healthier diet for your family. Better cultural and material rest. Health, qualified medical care and checkups. Travel. Investment for the future: education, gold, deposits, stocks. And so on. Because you have a loan, you are giving up these opportunities.

In economics, as we said, this is called **opportunity cost**. First, there is no free lunch. Even if I take you out for plov with my own money, you still spend one hour of your time. During that hour, you could have been working, earning money, learning something, or resting.

Second, once you direct a limited resource - your monthly income - to one place, you have fewer resources left for other places. This is a very powerful concept. It works at the level of a person, a company, a government, and society.

For example, why is watching short videos so harmful? Because our time and energy are limited. If you spend 1-2 hours every day on Instagram reels that bring you little benefit, you drain not only your time but also your mental energy.

You could have used that time and energy for deep work, learning a new language, or improving your life. Now you have less of that opportunity. That means the cost of reels is not only time and energy, but also the opportunities you could have achieved with that time and energy.

Companies have limited budgets. If a company spends its income on dividends for investors, it has fewer opportunities to improve its technologies and expand its operations. If a government increases spending on defense and military weapons, it has fewer opportunities for public education, healthcare, and ecology.

A loan works the same way. By taking a loan, you reduce your other opportunities. That is why the cost of a loan is not only **the interest you pay**, but also **the things you could have done with that money**.

## Psychological Cost

This cost is not discussed much in the media. Banks try to convince you that it does not exist. And it is not shown in any repayment schedule or financial plan. But ordinary borrowers know this cost very well.

First, a borrower carries a debt burden. There is a debt obligation. That is why a borrower feels more stress and anxiety. [According to some research](https://www.health.org.uk/evidence-hub/money/anxiety-and-problem-debt), people with debt are up to three times more likely to experience anxiety than people without debt. Why?

Legally, after taking a loan, **you are not free**. You are legally responsible to the bank. On the monthly payment date, the payment must be made. A late payment means not only penalties and collection, but also a [worse credit score](/en/loan/credit-score/). In addition, the bank automatically withdraws money from your plastic card as payment. Why do people avoid keeping money on the card linked to a loan? Why do people with loans usually keep their money in cash?

Second, loan repayment is mandatory. Whether you want it or not. Before almost everything else: before food, utility bills, your child's textbook, necessary medicine. If you do not pay the loan, your debts keep growing.

This means you have less money left for the things you want. People with loans probably sometimes have this thought: _"I wish I could do X/Y/Z if I did not have a loan"_. This also affects a person badly.

Third, anything can happen in life. You can get sick, lose your job, face suddenly higher expenses, and so on. The bank does not care: the contract is signed, the money is given, and now you must pay it back.

This obligation changes your psychology. It brings both fear and anxiety. Everyone worries about financial problems from time to time, and that is natural. People without a loan may think, _"All right, I have a lot of expenses next month, I need to earn money."_ People with a loan think, _"All right, the loan payment is coming, I also have all these expenses, what am I supposed to do?"_

The risk of being fired now feels different. Keeping your job stable becomes a priority. It becomes harder to say "no" to managers. You compromise more. You try to take fewer risks.

---

The psychological cost of a loan exists. For the borrower, it comes as stress, anxiety, and sometimes fear. But for many people, taking a loan means losing freedom. Freedom is not measured in money.

## Conclusion

Before taking a loan, answer three questions:

**1. What is the money cost?** - calculate the total interest. Use a calculator.

**2. What is the opportunity cost?** - what else could this money do?

**3. Are you ready for the psychological cost?** - how will a mandatory monthly payment change your life?

How much does the contract you are signing really cost? Is the loan worth all of this? In many cases, no, it is not worth it. Loans taken for many goals, especially consumer loans, are bad for the person who takes them. Is an iPhone 17 bought on credit worth all of this?

But a loan is not only a bad tool. Objectively, a loan can justify itself. It can be a good tool. But only in a few cases. That is for the next post.
