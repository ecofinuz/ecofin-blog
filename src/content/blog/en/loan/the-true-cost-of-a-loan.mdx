Imagine you are taking a loan from a bank. In the loan agreement, you see the [interest rate](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/), the principal, the interest to be paid, and the repayment term. The terms might look like this: a 20 million soum loan at 36% interest for one year. You do a rough calculation in your head. Then you sign the documents, formalize the loan, and receive the money. You think the calculation is over, and you move on with your life.

But the cost of a loan is not only the interest you pay. Many people never think about this. Of course, many people, especially banks, do not talk about it either. In reality, the true cost of the debt you take has three parts: **money cost**, **opportunity cost**, and **psychological cost**.

Let us look at them one by one and see how much a loan really costs your life. After this post, think about it: do you really need the loan?

## Money Cost

A loan has three main parts: **loan term**, **interest rate**, and **principal**. Its interest is calculated in two main ways: [annuity and declining balance](/en/loan/how-loans-work/). For example, car loan terms might look like this: 100 million soums for 5 years at 28% annually, using the annuity method. In that case, you pay around 3,100,000 soums per month on average.

That monthly payment is the monetary cost of the loan.

In Uzbekistan, almost all loans work as an _annuity_. That means you pay the same amount every month until the end of the loan term. Below, you can see how this schedule works. This table does not include bank fees or possible penalties:

When people talk about loan payments, they usually mean these interest payments. You borrow money, return more than you borrowed, and the difference becomes the bank's income. This number is easy to see in documents, advertisements, and websites. That is why the financial cost of a loan is easy to calculate.

Financial gurus focus exactly on this part. They call people to look for loans at the lowest possible rates.

But there are hidden costs of a loan that most people ignore. For the borrower, these are costs from the side of _opportunity_ and _psychology_. If analyzed properly, their value is not smaller than the financial cost, and may even be larger.

## Opportunity Cost

Loans are widespread in our society. Everyone probably has such a friend or acquaintance.

> Lend me some money until payday. I need to close my loan payment. Otherwise there will be a penalty, and the bank will start calling and looking for me. I do not want it to go to the enforcement bureau.

A loan is a contract that, for you, is debt. In any situation, it takes first priority.

Every month, when the payment date comes, you must pay the loan. First. In theory, after covering basic needs such as food, clothing, transport, and utilities, you pay the loan. Otherwise, the bank charges daily penalties and fines. Collection employees call you. If you still do not pay, the case can be sent to the enforcement bureau, and collection may reach the court. That is how the law works.

This process is **opportunity cost ([opportunity cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost))**. How?

Every family or individual has money they can control each month. Some have more, some have less. And each month, that money can be spent on many different things. If your obligations, meaning living expenses and the loan, increase, the amount of money you can freely use decreases.

For example, suppose you took a car loan for a BYD Yuan Up and must pay 5 million soums on the 10th of every month. Let us say that after basic needs, you have 10 million soums left. Automatically, 5 million soums goes to the loan. Whether you want it or not.

Think about it: what else could you use that 5 million for? Many things. Better education for your child. A healthier diet for your family. Better cultural and material rest. Health, qualified medical care and checkups. Travel. Investment for the future: education, gold, deposits, and stocks. And so on. Because you have a loan, you are giving up these opportunities.

Understand this correctly: after taking a loan, you are not free. You have an obligation before the bank and the government. And they have the legal right to force collection of the loan. Even by selling your house.

---

In economics, as we said, this is called **opportunity cost**. First, there is no free lunch. Even if I take you out for plov with my own money, you still spend one hour of your time. During that hour, you could have been working, earning money, learning something, or resting.

Second, once you direct a limited resource, your monthly income, to one place, you have fewer resources left for other places. This is a very powerful concept. It works at the level of a person, a company, a government, and society.

The government of Uzbekistan has limited time and money, believe me. The government has to allocate money to the most important areas: education, development, defense, infrastructure, and foreign affairs. If the state increases spending on defense and military weapons, it reduces its opportunities for public education, healthcare, and ecology. The Aral Desert problem in particular brings our state major problems and difficult decisions.

Companies have limited budgets. If a company spends its income on dividends for investors, it has fewer opportunities to improve its technologies and expand its operations.

Let us look at an example from ordinary life. Why is watching short videos, or _shorts_, so harmful? Because our time and energy are limited. If you spend 1-2 hours every day on **Instagram reels** that bring you no benefit, you drain not only your time, but also the energy in your brain.

That time and energy could have been used for deep work, learning a new language, going to the gym, or improving your life. Now you have less of that opportunity. This means the cost of reels is not only time and energy, but also the opportunities you could have achieved with that time and energy.

A loan works the same way. By taking a loan, you reduce your other opportunities. That is why the cost of a loan is not only **the interest you pay**, but also **the things you could have done with that money**.

## Psychological Cost

This cost is almost never discussed in the media. Banks try to convince you that it does not exist. Dear TBC Bank says this:

> We make your life easier. Online loans up to 100,000,000 soums. Apply - the answer comes in 1 minute. [Source](https://tbcbank.uz/product/kredity/)

And it runs this kind of PR campaign.

> You are your dreams 🚀 Big dreams - meant for everyone - continue to grow across our products, partnerships, and initiatives, helping more people move closer to what they aspire to. Because the scale of your life is shaped by the scale of your dreams. [Source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/tbcuzbekistan_tbcbank-bigdreams-brandplatform-activity-7444395589125017600-1kFZ?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAAD7x8YYB_2oUZlu5ES3JORHZDmJvM3ANFQ0)

Uzum, the local unicorn, advertises like this:

> If you have an Uzum card, you have money! Top up and transfer money for free. If your balance is not enough, get up to 25,000,000 soums on your card. No extra payments for 30 days. [Source](https://uzumbank.uz/uz/card/)

Banks frame credit psychologically and present it as dreams. Instead of showing risks, interest, and payments, they idealize credit and show it in a romantic style. This misleads ordinary people.

In Uzbekistan, if the economically active population that earns income is 15.1 million, then one third of them, or 5 million people, have at least one loan. The [Central Bank reported this on its Telegram channel](https://t.me/centralbankuzbekistan/11699). This is a frightening situation.

The situation has reached a point where the government has slowly started [regulating credit advertising](https://www.uzdaily.uz/en/uzbekistan-tightens-regulations-on-credit-advertising/).

Banks present credit to you through romance and motivational language. The psychological problems that come after credit are not shown in any repayment schedule or financial plan. But ordinary indebted people feel this cost very deeply once it falls on their shoulders.

First, a borrower carries a debt burden. There is a debt obligation. That is why a borrower feels more stress and anxiety. [According to some research](https://www.health.org.uk/evidence-hub/money/anxiety-and-problem-debt), people with debt are up to three times more likely to experience anxiety than people without debt. Why?

Legally, after taking a loan, **you are not free**. You are legally responsible to the bank. On the monthly payment date, the payment must be made. The bank does not care even a little about your family situation, health, or plans.

A late payment means not only penalties and collection, but also a [worse credit score](/en/loan/credit-score/). In addition, the bank automatically withdraws money from your plastic card as payment. Why do people avoid keeping money on the card linked to a loan? If you pay attention, indebted people withdraw their salary in cash as soon as it arrives.

Second, loan repayment is mandatory. Whether you want it or not. Before almost everything: before food, utility bills, your child's textbook, necessary medicine. If you do not pay the loan, your debts keep growing.

This means you have less money left for the goals you want. Sometimes none at all. People with loans probably sometimes have this thought: _"I wish I could do X/Y/Z if I did not have a loan."_ This also harms the human psyche.

Third, anything can happen in life. You can get sick, lose your job, face suddenly higher expenses, and so on. The bank does not care: the contract is signed, the money is given, and now you must pay it.

This obligation changes your psychology. It brings both fear and anxiety. Everyone worries about financial problems from time to time, and that is natural. People without a loan may think, _"All right, I have a lot of expenses next month, I need to earn money."_ People with a loan think, _"All right, the loan payment is coming, I also have all these expenses, what should I do?"_

The risk of being fired now feels different. Keeping your job stable becomes a priority. It becomes harder to say "no" to managers. You compromise more. You try to take fewer risks.

Taken together, all of this brings you stress, and after stress come blood pressure, heart attacks, and nervous system illnesses. There is your bright life built on credit, and dreams bought with debt.

---

The psychological cost of a loan exists. For the borrower, it comes as stress, anxiety, and sometimes fear. But for many people, taking a loan means losing financial freedom. Freedom is not measured in money.

## Conclusion

Before taking a loan, answer three questions:

**1. What is the money cost?** - calculate the total interest. Use a calculator.

**2. What is the opportunity cost?** - what else could this money do?

**3. Are you ready for the psychological cost?** - how will a mandatory monthly payment change your life?

How much does the contract you are signing really cost? Is the loan worth all of this? In many cases, no, it is not worth it. Loans taken for many goals, especially consumer loans, are bad for the person who takes them. Is an iPhone 17 bought on credit worth all this irritation? Is it worth all this anxiety?

But a loan is not only a bad tool. Objectively, [a loan can justify itself](/en/loan/when-debt-works/). It can be a good tool. But only in a few very specific cases. That is for the next post.
