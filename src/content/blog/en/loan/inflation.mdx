## What Is Inflation?

**Inflation** is the general rise in the prices of goods and services over time. In other words, it is the decline of money's purchasing power. There is a big difference between 1,000,000 soums in 2026 and 1,000,000 soums in 2020. Over six years, the purchasing power of money has fallen noticeably.

[Bank.uz defines inflation](https://jet.bank.uz/article/inflyatsiya-80) like this:

> Inflation is the steady rise in the general prices of goods and services. During inflation, some goods may become significantly more expensive, others may become cheaper, and some prices may stay unchanged.

Uzbek stand-up comedian Mirshakar Fayzulloyev has a joke:

> Ten years ago, if I entered Korzinka with 200,000 soums, I could not carry all the bags. Now I walk out shaking one tiny bag.

In more formal language, during inflation the nominal value of money - the number printed on it - does not change, but its real value, or purchasing power, falls.

Inflation does not happen simply because time passes. That is a major misconception. In economics, if the amount of money rises relative to goods, money loses value. Serious political or economic mistakes can lead to [hyperinflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation).

Did you know that Zimbabwe had trillionaires because of inflation?

![100 trillion Zimbabwe banknote](/images/posts/inflyatsiya/Zimbabwe_$100_trillion_2009.jpg)

**100 trillion Zimbabwe dollars.**

Calculating inflation actually takes a lot of time. Tracking prices across the whole economy and tracking people's choices of goods is not easy. All countries use the concept of a "[consumer basket (CPI)](https://jet.bank.uz/article/inflyatsiya-80#block-index-1)" to monitor inflation.

Inflation is a very large topic and deserves its own post. In fact, a dedicated series about inflation will be written soon.

For now, we will discuss the basic concepts of inflation and its effect on loans.

---

Inflation is usually measured with two indicators: **month-over-month** compared with the previous month, and **annual** compared with the same month of the previous year. These make it easy to calculate inflation between two periods.

Inflation calculators work the same way. The Central Bank of Uzbekistan publishes this data [every month](https://cbu.uz/en/monetary-policy/annual-inflation/indicators/).

## How Does It Work?

Prices rise for several reasons. The two main ones are:

**Demand-pull inflation** happens when there is a lot of money in the economy and too few goods. Imagine Uzbekistan's national football team plays the United Arab Emirates at Bunyodkor Stadium. The stadium holds a maximum of 34,000 spectators. Millions want to watch the match, and hundreds of thousands want to go to the stadium.

Demand is many times higher than the number of tickets. In that situation, ticket prices naturally rise. A limited number of goods is wanted by many people. A ticket with an original price of 100,000 soums rises according to demand and stops rising only when it reaches a certain price.

That is the theory. In official ticket offices, however, prices do not change. Instead, street resellers buy tickets cheaply and sell them at a markup. Economically, it makes sense; morally, it does not. People who travel to the regions by taxi before holidays know this too: there are few cars and many passengers, so fares naturally rise.

This same mechanism is why gold prices do not simply fall. Gold is a limited resource, but demand for it is high. Governments, companies, and wealthy people store wealth in gold. That is why personal finance experts recommend buying some gold for the future.

At the national level, if the Central Bank issues a lot of money or credit becomes cheaper ([currently 14%](https://cbu.uz/uz/monetary-policy/refinancing-rate/)), people and businesses have more money and buy more. Aggregate demand rises. But production does not always change quickly, so prices rise. Governments have better mechanisms for controlling this type of inflation. We will discuss that in other articles.

---

**Cost-push inflation** happens when production becomes more expensive. It is important because it happens when the prices of basic goods change: fuel, raw materials, food, or labor. If these costs rise, businesses add them to prices.

For example, after the war in Iran began in February, the Strait of Hormuz was closed. About 20% of the world's oil was stuck in the blockade. As a result, oil prices rose sharply. This affected goods and services worldwide. Airline ticket prices also increased.

In Uzbekistan, higher methane or gasoline prices affect all goods: every family and business needs fuel. Production, logistics, taxis - everything becomes more expensive. Demand-pull inflation mainly affects a particular sector, while cost-push inflation affects the whole economy. Unfortunately, governments do not have strong tools against this type of inflation.

## How It Affects Ordinary People

Inflation does not affect everyone equally. It is good for borrowers, because the nominal value of the debt does not change while its real value falls.

But inflation hits these three groups especially hard:

**#1. People holding cash savings.** Money kept in a box or in a low-interest account loses value over time. This is true even if it is in US dollars. The US dollar also experiences inflation, like any currency. People keep money in dollars because it loses value more slowly than the soum and is more trusted.

This also explains why people in Uzbekistan are so attached to real estate. A person with money looks for a reliable, inflation-resistant investment. Unfortunately, the stock market is still not well developed in our country. The culture of buying and storing gold is also not well formed. Partnership-based business and investment, such as mudarabah, is still not popular. The only remaining familiar options are land and housing.

**#2. People whose salaries are not indexed.** If your salary grows more slowly than inflation, you are actually earning less - even if your monthly pay increased. Suppose annual inflation averages 10%, but your monthly income grows by 8%. In reality, you are losing money. Inflation strongly affects low-ranking public-sector workers.

**#3. People with fixed income.** Pensions, benefits, or fixed rental income do not automatically rise with inflation. Why does the government raise pensions, benefits, and stipends by 10%-15% every year? Inflation is one of the main reasons.

Who benefits from inflation? [Borrowers](/en/loan/when-debt-works/) - if the interest rate is fixed.

This usually happens between two individuals. Suppose you lent a relative 10,000,000 soums in January 2025 and received it back in January 2026. Based on inflation, that money should have been around 10,700,000 soums.

People usually do not index personal debt to inflation. Because money loses value, you lose money by lending.

## Loans and Inflation

So, how is inflation connected to loans?

The connection between the two is unclear to many people. But it is not difficult to understand. Inflation determines whether [interest rates](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/) are low or high.

Suppose you took a 10,000,000 soum loan in 2020. Over five years, inflation averaged around 11%. That means the soums you repay are smaller in real value than the soums from 2020. The bank receives the same nominal amount, but its purchasing power has fallen.

That is why banks account for inflation when setting interest rates. In simple mathematical terms, the Fisher formula says:

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">r = R - π</p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        R - nominal rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; π - inflation &nbsp;·&nbsp; r - real rate
    </p>
</div>

If a loan is issued at 24% and inflation is 10%, the bank actually earns about 14% in real terms. In Uzbekistan, the Central Bank currently keeps the policy rate [at 14%](https://cbu.uz/uz/monetary-policy/refinancing-rate/). Inflation is around 7%. That is profitable for the bank and expensive for the borrower.

When TBC Bank gives you a consumer loan at 49%, by this logic it earns 42% in real terms. In reality, the full Fisher formula also includes risk. We will look at that in the article [how interest rates are set](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/).
