## What Is a Consumer Loan?

**A consumer loan** is debt given by a bank or microfinance institution to an individual for a fixed term and at an [interest rate](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/). **For consumption.**

Consumer loans are different from business loans. Business loans are issued to start, expand, or develop a business. A consumer loan is issued so a person can spend the money on something.

For example, a car, home appliances, education. Sometimes it is just cash. The purpose can differ, but the mechanism is the same. Unfortunately, people also take loans for mobile phones, travel, and weddings.

Except for a few cases, a consumer loan will [cost you a lot](/en/loan/when-debt-works/).

Unlike business, a very large share of consumer borrowers take loans only to spend the money. Buying a new gas stove on a 12-month loan will not increase your income. Your skills will not grow either. It may bring convenience, but it will not bring profit.

Taking a loan for a wedding is one of the biggest financial mistakes: not only do you waste at least 100 million soums in a single day, you also return that money with interest.

A consumer loan is useful only when it is used for the right purpose. For education, new skills, or a new job. But borrowing at a high interest rate only for consumption is financially wrong. [Loans should always be the last option](/en/loan/when-debt-works/).

The laptop I am writing this post on was also bought by my brother on a $700 loan five years ago. There was no other option at the time. Through it, we gained new knowledge, learned a profession, and found work. My brother even worked in Germany for a year. Today, buying laptops like that is no longer a problem, thankfully.

Now you have seen what a consumer loan is. Next, we will look at what a loan consists of. Its components have a major effect on loan calculations.

---

A loan has three main parameters: **principal** (the amount you borrowed), **interest rate** (the price of the loan), and **term** (the repayment period). These three numbers determine your monthly payment and the total amount you will repay.

In Uzbekistan, consumer loans are repaid under two schedules: **annuity** and **declining balance**. Annuity means equal monthly payments: you pay the same amount every month. Declining balance means you pay more at the beginning and less toward the end.

## Annuity

**Annuity** is a loan schedule where you pay the same amount every month. The payment does not change from the first month to the last. This is the most common loan type in Uzbekistan. You know exactly how much you will pay every month and on which date. For example, you may pay 4,000,000 soums for an auto loan on the 10th of every month.

[Bank.uz describes annuity](https://jet.bank.uz/article/annuitet-yoki-differensial-tolov-135#block-index-1) this way:

> Annuity payments are made in the same amount every month, but the ratio of principal and interest inside each payment changes. At the beginning of the loan, interest is higher and principal is lower. Toward the end, the opposite happens: principal is higher and interest is lower.

Since you are studying financial literacy, remember this: banks are greedy; in fact, the whole financial sector is [greedy](/en/loan/credit-is-not-neutral/). The Western financial system was built this way over centuries, and after independence we copied much of it.

But "same payment" does not mean the internal structure is the same. The principal is paid down every month, and monthly interest is calculated based on that. Under the annuity schedule, in the first months a large share of the payment is interest and a small share is principal.

In other words, at the beginning you mostly pay interest, not the debt itself. It could have been the opposite: a larger principal part and a smaller interest part. Then the total amount you pay would be lower. But that is not how it works.

Over time, the ratio changes. By the final month, almost the entire payment is principal. _Nothing personal, just business._

In Excel, annuity principal and interest parts can be calculated with `=PPMT()` and `=IPMT()`. Mathematically, the annuity formula looks like this:

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">
        M = P × r(1 + r)<sup>n</sup> / ((1 + r)<sup>n</sup> - 1)
    </p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        P - principal &nbsp;·&nbsp; r - monthly interest rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; n -
        number of months &nbsp;·&nbsp; M - monthly payment
    </p>
</div>

After all, the iPhone is important. This thing will bring him respect and prestige. Toshmat gets an online loan in five minutes and pays 990,000 soums per month.

The monthly payment is not that large, right? But over 24 months, he returns almost **24,000,000 soums** for the 15,000,000 soums he borrowed. **A full 60% extra!** That is before counting penalties for even one late day.

## Declining Balance

Under the **declining balance**, or differentiated, schedule, the principal is divided into equal parts and paid every month. Interest is calculated on the remaining debt each month, so as the balance falls, the interest amount also falls. This is the second common loan calculation method.

[Bank.uz describes differentiated payments](https://jet.bank.uz/article/annuitet-yoki-differensial-tolov-135#block-index-2) this way:

> A differentiated payment is another loan repayment system where the borrower pays different amounts every month, and those amounts decrease each time.

Because you pay the principal first, the interest payment is also lower than under annuity. Unlike annuity, you are paying the debt itself, not mainly interest. That is why the early payment is higher than annuity, but the total interest paid is lower.

Mathematically, the declining balance logic is much simpler:

<div class="formula-block">
    <p class="formula-label">Formula</p>
    <p class="formula">
        Principal part = P / n<br />
        Interest part = Balance × r<br />
        Monthly payment = Principal part + Interest part
    </p>
    <p class="formula-note">
        P - principal &nbsp;·&nbsp; r - monthly interest rate &nbsp;·&nbsp; n -
        number of months &nbsp;·&nbsp; Balance - remaining debt for that month
    </p>
</div>

Returning to the example above: under the declining balance method, Toshmat pays around 22,500,000 soums in total. But for the first 10 months, he pays more than 1,000,000 soums per month. His principal part stays fixed at 625,000 soums per month, while the interest keeps falling.

Banks mostly offer annuity because they want to earn as much profit as possible. For example, our dear TBC Bank does not offer you any [declining balance loan](<https://tbcbank.uz/product/kredity/tariff/#:~:text=Annuitet%20(teng%20miqdordagi%20to%27lovlar%20bilan)>).

## Which One Is Better?

For the bank, both are good. For you, honestly, it does not make a massive difference. In both cases, you return the loan with interest.

The same principal, interest rate, and term produce different results under the two schedules. The main difference is the total interest paid and the way the monthly payment changes.

| Annuity                                                   | Declining balance                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Monthly payment is **the same**. Budgeting is easier.     | Monthly payment decreases over time. Heavier at first.      |
| Total interest is **higher**. Convenient for the bank.    | Total interest is **lower**. Better for the borrower.       |
| **Very common** in Uzbekistan.                            | More common in **mortgage** and **corporate** loans.        |

If monthly payment stability matters to you, choose annuity. If you want to pay less interest and can afford higher early payments, choose declining balance. Which one is right for you depends on your situation.

In practice, most banks offer annuity because it brings the bank more interest. If you do not ask, they will not offer declining balance.

When taking a loan, make sure to ask about declining balance. Also understand the principal, term, and interest rate clearly. Then you can use the tables above to calculate it yourself and make an independent decision.
