## What Is a Credit Score?

**A credit score** is a numerical indicator that shows how responsibly you meet your obligations to financial institutions.

In Uzbekistan, this score ranges from **100 to 500**. The higher the number, the more reliable you are considered as a borrower. A reliable borrower means lower risk for the bank, so the interest rate can be lower.

This relationship works in one direction: your credit score affects your interest rate, but the interest rate itself does not affect your credit score.

## How Does It Work?

In Uzbekistan, credit history is maintained by the **Credit Information Bureau**. All licensed banks and microfinance organizations send information to the bureau.

When you take a loan, pay it on time, or miss a payment, all of that is stored in the bureau's database. Your credit score is formed based on this information.

The main factors that affect the score are:

- Paying on time
- Number and total amount of existing loans
- Length of credit history
- Late payments and how long they remained overdue

The bureau does not fully disclose its algorithm. But the factors above are universal for almost all credit bureaus.

## How To Check It

Citizens of Uzbekistan have the right to receive their credit history **once a year for free**.

To do this:

1. Go to **my.gov.uz**
2. Search for the "Credit history" service
3. Authenticate with an electronic signature or One ID
4. Submit the request

The result is provided electronically. It shows all active and closed loans, payment history, and your credit score.

## What If Your Score Is Low?

You cannot raise a credit score quickly. It is a process that takes time.

But there are several practical steps:

**Pay existing loans on time.** This is the most influential factor. Even one month of delay can hurt your score.

**Close overdue payments as soon as possible.** If the debt remains, it can be recorded as a new negative event every month.

**Avoid taking new loans.** Every credit application affects the score. Applying to several lenders in a short period sends a negative signal.

**Be patient.** A good credit history is built over years.

## Misunderstanding Credit Scores

Many people understand a credit score only as "creditworthiness." In reality, its role is expanding. Some rental agreements, employers, and even insurance pricing may take credit history into account.

No credit history can also be a problem. If you have never taken a loan, the Credit Information Bureau has no information about you. For the bank, that creates uncertainty, which means risk. Sometimes zero credit history is treated as worse than a low score.

## Conclusion

A credit score answers the first question a bank asks about you: "Will this person repay the debt?"

Remember the interest rate formula: the risk premium was the largest component. Your credit score is what determines that risk premium personally for you.
