## What Is a Credit Score?

Finance has a lot of analytical ratios. How much profit was made: return on investment, net profit margin. How well a company is operating: cash conversion cycle. How responsibly a firm treats society and nature: ESG rating. A credit score, or credit scoring, is one of those analytical indicators.

**A credit score** is a numerical indicator that shows how reliable a borrower is. It may sound complicated, but the idea is quite simple.

Imagine you have two friends. Let the first one be Toshmat. His work situation is unclear: one day he is in one _hustle_, the next day in another. He is always in debt, yet he has the newest phone in his hand and spends every month at Charvak. He loves videos with lines like "do not play with a man's luck."

The second one is Eshmat. He is educated and works at a good place. He is settled, spends money carefully, and you have never heard anything shameful about him. Other than the mortgage he took for his home, he has no loans.

Now imagine both come to you on the same day and ask to borrow 2,000,000 soums. Naturally, you would hesitate to lend money to Toshmat. Questions appear right away: "Will I get my money back? When will I get it back? Will I give it with my hands and then chase it with my feet?"

With Eshmat, no such questions come up. He has a job, a car, and a home. Your trust is high. His reputation is strong. You lend him money without extra questions because you trust that he will return it.

A credit score is the numerical expression of that trust. Toshmat's score would be lower, Eshmat's would be higher.

The only difference is that a bank does not personally know the people who come for loans. So it takes their workplace, salary, credit history, whether they paid previous loans on time, assets, criminal history, and other factors, then turns all of that into a single credit score.

In Uzbekistan, this work is done by [KATM](https://infokredit.uz/) (the Credit Information Analytical Center) and the international firm [CRIF](https://www.crif.uz/). For several good reasons, credit scoring algorithms are not disclosed to the public.

## Credit Scoring And Loans

[As we said in the previous post](/en/loan/how-rates-are-determined/), the interest rate on a loan consists of three factors: [inflation](/en/loan/inflation/), the risk premium, and the profit margin. Credit scoring directly affects the risk premium.

The better your credit score, the lower the interest rate you should theoretically receive. This does not affect consumer loans that much. It is a more noticeable factor for large companies and mortgages.

But if your credit score is bad, you may be offered a very high rate. Or you may not be approved at all. Because credit scoring is aggregated at the state level, banks can see your score. In other words, if you take a 49% loan from dear TBC Bank and then run away without paying it, SQB Bank and Kapital Bank may also know about it if you come to them for another loan.

Because this system is standardized by the state, banks trust your rating and use it when deciding whether to lend.

## How Does It Work?

Credit rating algorithms are kept strictly secret. The reason is that if the factors and their weights were public, _clever_ people would manipulate their scores. That would reduce banks' trust in credit scoring, raise the risk premium, and push interest rates higher for ordinary borrowers. That is why credit scoring algorithms are kept confidential.

Still, the general factors are known. The most important one is credit history. More important than salary, assets, and current debts. Why? Because in Uzbekistan, especially in the regions, many people do not have official jobs or official income: market sellers, craftsmen, mechanics, small private entrepreneurs. In many Uzbek families, homes and cars are registered under the parents' names. Uzbeks also usually do not formalize personal debts with a notary. For these reasons, salary, assets, and current debts are not always useful factors.

This is also why the Uzbek government has strongly pushed the move from cash to electronic plastic card payments. For taxes, for economic indicators, and for oversight. More on that in other posts.

---

Historically, credit scoring models placed heavy emphasis on history. That is why credit history plays such a large role. Even if you have a lot of money, if you have no credit history, your credit score will not be good. Today, however, other factors also receive more attention: turnover on plastic cards, employment history, age, gender, assets, and so on.

Credit scoring is updated and developed every year. As Uzbekistan's population moves from cash to plastic card payments, the algorithms will keep changing too.

## How To Get Your Score

So, we have looked at the credit rating system. How does it work in Uzbekistan, and more importantly, how can you get your own score?

In Uzbekistan, credit scoring uses a 0-500 scale and is divided into 5 classes:

- 0-100 (E class)
- 100-200 (D class)
- 200-300 (C class)
- 300-400 (B class)
- 400-500 (A class)

The higher your score, the better. I have a job, official income, and taxes, but I have never taken a loan. And I do not plan to. My score came out as 281.

To get your own credit score:

1. Go to the **my.gov.uz** portal
2. Search for the "Issuance of a credit history certificate" service. [Here is the link](https://my.gov.uz/en/service/411).
3. Authenticate with an electronic signature or One ID
4. Submit the request

The result is provided as an HTML file inside a .zip archive, for security and convenience for banks. It shows all active and closed loans, payment history, and your credit score.

## Low Score?

If your credit score is low and getting a loan is difficult, the best advice is simple: try to pay your loans on time. In the long run, try not to take loans at all.

In practice, you cannot raise a credit score quickly. It is a process that takes time.

But there are several practical steps:

- **Pay existing loans on time.** This is the most influential factor.
  Even one month of delay hurts your score.
- **Close overdue payments as soon as possible.** If the debt remains, it is
  recorded as a new negative entry every month.
- **Avoid taking new loans.** Every credit application affects your score.
  Applying to several places in a short period sends a negative signal.
- **Be patient.** A good credit history is built over years.
- **Become wealthier.** Try to reach a point where you do not need credit. Financial freedom, especially for a man, is one of the greatest freedoms. Not being trapped by credit feels good.

## Conclusion

A credit score answers the first question a bank asks about you:
"Will this person repay the debt?"

Remember the interest rate formula: ((1 + R) = (1 + r) x (1 + m) x (1 + pi)). The risk premium was the largest component. Your credit score is exactly what determines that risk premium - personally for you.
